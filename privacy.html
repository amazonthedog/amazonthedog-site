Privacy

What this site keeps track of.

amazonthedog.com is a small site about our dog. The site itself doesn't take payments, run ads, or ask for your name or email.

Visit counts

We use Google Analytics to see how the site gets used: how many people stop by and where they came from, how far they scroll and how long each part stays on screen, which buttons, links and photos they tap (like "Follow on Instagram"), which gallery photos they open, heart or send to a friend, whether they copy or print something (never what), how fast the page loads, and any errors. Google Analytics uses cookies to do this. We never send it your name, email, anything you type or copy, or anything else that identifies you. If you visit from the EU, the UK, or Switzerland, the site doesn't set analytics cookies.

Hearts in the photo gallery

The hearts you tap in the gallery are saved only in your own browser, so they're still there when you come back. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes them. "Send to a friend" uses your device's own share menu, or copies the photo's link.

Her music player

The song player on the homepage and the music page comes from SoundCloud. It loads from SoundCloud's own servers, so SoundCloud may set its own cookies and see that you visited, under SoundCloud's privacy policy. We count which songs get played, how far into each song people listen, how many seconds they listen, and when someone opens a song's share menu, using the song's title only, in Google Analytics, the same way as the other visit counts above.

Links to other sites

The follow buttons open Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, Snapchat, LinkedIn, and SoundCloud. Her Christmas book's download and checkout happen on Gumroad, so Gumroad handles any email address or payment you give it there. The calendar buttons open Google Calendar or your own calendar app, and "Directions" opens Google Maps. Each of those sites' own privacy policies apply once you're there.

Opting out

You can block Google Analytics on every site with Google's opt-out browser add-on, a content blocker, or your browser's privacy settings. You can read how Google uses this data at policies.google.com.
